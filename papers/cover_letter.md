Dear Editor-in-Chief,

We are pleased to submit our manuscript, **"Modeling the impact of outbreak response immunization on typhoid fever using data from 19 outbreaks between 2000–2022"**, for consideration for publication in *Vaccine*.

Despite the availability of licensed typhoid conjugate vaccines (TCVs), outbreak response immunization (ORI) remains severely underutilized — Zimbabwe is the only country to have received Gavi support for a TCV ORI campaign. A key barrier has been the absence of modeling evidence quantifying the potential impact of reactive TCV deployment across diverse real-world outbreaks. This study fills that gap.

We applied a static vaccine impact model to 19 typhoid outbreaks reported between 2000 and 2022, estimating cases and deaths averted, AMR burden averted, and cost-effectiveness across a range of timing and coverage scenarios. Key findings include:

- At an 8-week delay and 80% coverage, ORI reduces cases by a median of 30.0% (95%CI: 0%–68.5%), averting a median of 275 cases per outbreak.
- Impact is strongly time-sensitive: a 1-week delay yields 66.9% case reduction, approximately halving by week 7.
- Longer-duration and AMR-positive outbreaks benefit most, underscoring the value of context-specific prioritization.
- Results are robust to assumptions about indirect vaccine effectiveness.

*Vaccine* has published influential typhoid vaccine modeling work (e.g., Burrows et al. 2023), and we believe this manuscript makes a distinct and timely contribution by focusing on the reactive immunization context with outbreak-level empirical data. The findings directly inform policy at Gavi, WHO, and national immunization programmes.

This manuscript is original and not under consideration elsewhere. The study uses publicly available data from published outbreak reports; no ethics approval was required. The authors declare no conflicts of interest.

We look forward to your consideration.
