## 1. The outbreak

A large, protracted waterborne typhoid outbreak in **Kampala, Uganda, January–June 2015** (Kabwama
et al. 2017, *BMC Public Health*). **10,230 suspected**, **1,038 probable** (TUBEX TF IgM-positive),
and **56 of 364 blood-cultured confirmed** (15% culture positivity). Key features:

- **Source: contaminated water and street-vended beverages** — locally made drinks (juice, "kabalagala"
  water) prepared with contaminated spring/surface water; a case–control study implicated drinking
  water and vended-drink exposure. Recurrent contamination sustained transmission **over ~6 months**,
  so the outbreak is **mixed, common-source dominant** (project source-decomposition θ = 0.60).
- **Predominantly working-age adults**: attack rate 12/1,000 in 15–59 y vs 2/1,000 in others.
- **Surveillance was actively enhanced mid-outbreak**: six treatment centres offering free diagnosis
  and treatment, plus media/local-leader mobilisation, opened around the early-February investigation.
  Kabwama attributes the mid-outbreak case surge partly to this ramp-up — visible in our data as the
  jump from ~30 cases/week (weeks 1–5) to hundreds from week 6 (Fig 1, dashed line).
- Complications included intestinal perforations; the outbreak overlaps the H58 MDR era.

Weekly series used (24 weeks, from the project's canonical time series): total **10,152**, peak
**1,997 at week 12**.

## 2. Modelling approach — three observation layers + transmission

The analysis is built as an **observation model** feeding a **transmission model**, so that the three
requested features (non-typhoid background, PPV propagation, asymptomatic infections) enter where they
belong.

**(a) PPV propagation & the non-typhoid background.** The suspected curve is decomposed as
`S_t = I_t (typhoid symptomatic) + B_t (non-typhoid background)`. The fitted community PPV posterior
(**π = 0.153, 90% CrI 0.044–0.421**, 6,000 draws) pins the cumulative split (`ΣI = π·ΣS`); typhoid is
recovered by additive de-backgrounding (`B = (1−π)·mean(S)`, held fixed). The background is a mixture
of **other bacterial (NTS/Shigella)** and **non-bacterial (malaria/viral)** febrile illness.

> **How the bacterial background is handled — and why it matters less than it seems.** TCV prevents
> *only* typhoid, so the entire non-typhoid background — whatever its bacterial composition — is
> **invariant to the vaccine**. Decomposing it into NTS vs non-bacterial (we carry an illustrative
> NTS share φ = 0.15) therefore does **not** change the vaccine-impact estimand; it changes only (i)
> the *interpretation* of the 85% non-typhoid fraction and (ii) the co-benefit attributable to
> **WASH** (which *would* reduce co-transmitted waterborne NTS/Shigella, unlike TCV). The one place
> composition could bite the transmission model is if the background were itself *epidemic* (a
> co-circulating NTS surge with its own dynamics); we test a flat vs. ramped background and find the
> impact conclusions unchanged (§4, reporting scenario).

**(b) Asymptomatic infections.** Symptomatic typhoid `I_t` is the observed (clinical) tip. Total
infections `T_t = I_t / p_symp` (base `p_symp = 0.40`), so **~60% of infections are asymptomatic and
unobserved**. Asymptomatic transmission is already embedded in the reconstructed `R_t` (which is
generated by the whole transmission network); the overlay partitions the burden (symptomatic +
asymptomatic + carriers) and scales the averted counts. Chronic carriers accrue at θ = 0.029 of
infections.

**(c) Transmission — and a diagnostic finding.** We reconstruct the typhoid `R_t` from the
de-backgrounded incidence and find a **peak R_t of ~13** (Fig 2) — far above any plausible propagated
typhoid `R_t` (2–4). This is **not an error but the common-source signature**: simultaneous
contaminated-water exposures are misread by a pure renewal model as explosive person-to-person
transmission, which would overstate the vaccine's *indirect* effect. We therefore use the
primary **additive source-plus-propagated renewal model**, with
$I_t=X_t+P_t$ inside one shared recursion and θ = 0.60 allocating baseline true typhoid between
the two components. Both components receive direct vaccine protection; propagated incidence also
declines through feedback. The former calculation
`averted = θ·static + (1−θ)·renewal` is retained only as a paired historical comparator.

Vaccine parameters (project config): coverage 0.80 base, direct VE ψ = 0.83, transmission VE
ψ_T = 0.8ψ, protection delay = campaign-request delay + 14-d campaign + 21-d immunity onset. Base
("production") protection week = **13**. Uncertainty is propagated over the 6,000 PPV draws.

## 3. Decomposition results (Table `tab_decomposition.csv`, Fig 1)

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| Suspected cases (observed) | 10,152 |
| **True typhoid, symptomatic (PPV)** | **1,550 [447, 4,273]** |
| Non-typhoid background (all) | 8,602 |
| — non-typhoid bacterial (~NTS/Shigella) | ~1,290 |
| — non-bacterial (malaria/viral) | ~7,312 |
| **Total typhoid infections (incl. asymptomatic)** | **3,875 [1,118, 10,684]** |
| — asymptomatic typhoid | ~2,325 |
| Chronic carriers formed (θ × infections) | ~112 |

So of ~10,000 "typhoid" suspected cases, **only ~1,550 are truly typhoid**, but the *true* burden —
counting the ~2,325 asymptomatic infections the surveillance never sees — is **~3,875 infections**.
The PPV correction and the asymptomatic correction pull in opposite directions and largely offset.

## 4. Vaccine-impact results

Both arms use the **same** vaccine efficacy (ψ = 0.83) — they differ only in whether indirect
effects compound — so the renewal ≥ static ordering (a clean bracket) holds by construction.

**Production timing (protection week 13, coverage 0.80):**

| Model | True typhoid reduction |
|---|---|
| Static (direct only, lower bracket) | 11.4% |
| Renewal (transmission, upper bracket) | 11.5% |
| **Additive source + propagated (θ = 0.60, primary)** | **11.4% → observed suspected 1.7%** |
| Historical θ-weighted comparator | 11.4% (paired difference +0.0 percentage points) |

The additive model averts **~177 symptomatic** and **~443 total** typhoid infections, including
~13 carriers. The
observed reduction a suspected-case surveillance system would record is only **1.7%** — the true
reduction diluted by the PPV (observed = π × true).

**Timing × coverage (Table `tab_impact_grid.csv`, Fig 3) — the headline (additive primary):**

| Protection week | cov 0.30 | 0.50 | 0.65 | 0.80 | 0.90 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **6 (fastest)** | 32% | 50% | 62% | **73%** | 80% |
| 9 | 32% | 49% | 61% | 72% | 79% |
| 11 | 18% | 30% | 38% | 46% | 52% |
| **13 (production)** | 4% | 7% | 9% | **11%** | 13% |
| 15 | ~0 | ~0 | ~0 | ~0 | ~0 |

At these timings the renewal (upper) runs from **84%** (week 6, cov 0.80) down to 12% (week 13), the
static (lower) from 66% to 11% — the gap is the indirect effect, largest for early response and ~0
post-peak.

**Response speed dominates coverage.** Protecting by week 6 averts ~73% of true typhoid at 80%
coverage; the *same* coverage at the realistic production week 13 averts only ~11%; by week 15 the
outbreak is over. Even 30% coverage delivered fast (32% at week 6) beats 90% coverage delivered at
production timing (13% at week 13).

**Scenario robustness:**
- *Time-varying reporting* (surveillance ramp at week 6): additive reduction ≈**11.4%**, essentially
  unchanged across pre-ramp reporting 1.0→0.5 — the impact conclusion is robust to the ramp (Table
  `tab_reporting_scenario.csv`).
- *Asymptomatic fraction* (`tab_asymptomatic_sweep.csv`): total infections 2,583–5,166 and total
  averted scale with p_symp (0.60→0.30) — burden scales, % reduction unchanged.
- *Observation model* (`tab_obs_model.csv`): additive at both levels (primary) 11.4% true → **1.7% observed**;
  multiplicative (reconstruct on suspected, observed = true, no dilution). The additive/multiplicative
  choice *is* the choice of whether surveillance sees the dilution.
- *Transmission-VE sensitivity*: setting transmission efficacy to ψ_T = 0.8ψ (rather than ψ)
  weakens compounding in the propagated component; it does not alter the source allocation and shifts
  the additive estimate by <1 point at production timing.

## 5. Insights

1. **PPV and asymptomatic corrections nearly cancel for burden, but not for surveillance.** True
   typhoid is only ~15% of suspected, yet total infections (~3,875) exceed the suspected typhoid label
   count because ~60% are asymptomatic. The corrections offset for *burden*; but for *surveillance
   visibility* they compound — a real 11.4% true reduction shows up as a 1.7% dip in suspected cases.
2. **Kabwama's R_t inflation is a feature, not a bug** — it flags the common-source dominance and is
   exactly why a pure renewal model would overstate ORI's indirect benefit here. The additive
   source-plus-propagated
   combination is the correct, and necessary, handling.
3. **This outbreak was essentially unwinnable at production speed.** At the realistic week-13
   protection time, ORI averts ~10% of true typhoid; the operational lesson (matching the manuscript's
   thesis) is that **speed, not coverage, is the binding constraint**, and that a campaign this late
   accrues most of its value against *future* seasons (the estimand-2/endemic question), not the index
   outbreak.
4. **The non-typhoid bacterial background does not change TCV impact** — only WASH would touch it — so
   its main role is to explain why culture-confirmation (or PPV correction) is essential before any
   surveillance-based ORI evaluation.

## 6. Caveats & next steps

- The additive de-background concentrates typhoid in the outbreak *excess*; at π = 0.15 this makes the
  typhoid curve sharp and inflates the raw renewal R_t. We handle this with smoothing for display
  and explicit source allocation inside the renewal recursion, but a **mechanistic SEIAR-C-W model**
  (explicit asymptomatic + carrier +
  environmental water compartment) fit directly to the curve is the natural next step to represent
  asymptomatic *transmission* (not just burden) and the waterborne reservoir — the `seiar_c()`
  scaffold is in `01_model.R`.
- `p_symp`, the NTS background share, and ψ_T are assumptions swept in sensitivity, not fitted.
- Estimand 2 (disease prevented over the vaccine-protection period, incl. Kampala's recurrent
  seasons) is not yet modelled — that is the ODE/endemic arm.
